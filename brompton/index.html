Recommendation

Weights below include your 240 g fork, Ti screws, light pedals and carbon mudguards, plus Schwalbe One tyres and a Ti seatpost. Change any of it in the calculator.

9.8kg
You use 1st and 6th on your M6L: BWR 6-speed

Most range (302%) of anything that fits your rear frame, and the hub is a stock Brompton part any dealer can service. Building it into a BrommiePlus wheelset barely changes the weight, because the hub is most of it. A third rear cog later gives 9 speeds (≈350%) for about 20 g.

9.2kg
You mostly ride the middle gears: Brompton 2-speed + Superlight front wheel

The lightest build that is still all-stock and close to maintenance-free, about 0.7 kg under the BWR. Range is only ≈135%, which is flat-city gearing.

BrommiePlus 3×2Same Sturmey BWR hub and same Brompton 2-speed parts as stock, so not meaningfully lighter. Lighter rims and front hub may save 50–100 g. Make sure the hub is the BWR (takes two sprockets); a plain Sturmey 3-speed only takes one.
Carbon forkAt 240 g it is 20–110 g lighter than a Brompton Ti fork. Good reviews help. It is still the one part where a crack means a crash, so torque it correctly, check it after any knock, and confirm the 349 brake reach before fitting.
Front discWorks, but adds ≈125 g and ≈€200–300 for a bike you want light. Worth it only if you ride a lot in rain or on long descents. Details below.
5-speed derailleurThe BrommiePlus Hubsmith wheelsets are the lightest proper option, but a 5-cog cassette only gives ≈190% range and the derailleur parts are mainly made for the newer P/T Line frame.
Not an optionBrompton's own 4- and 12-speed systems need the newer Advance rear frame. Your titanium rear frame is the older Superlight/C Line type.

Front disc brake

Your fork's disc mount makes it possible. For a bike built to be light it's hard to justify: it adds about 125 g and €200–300, and puts all the front braking load through one carbon fork leg. It makes sense if you often ride in the rain or down long hills, where Brompton rim brakes are weakest, or if you want a front rim without a brake track. Good pads on the stock caliper close much of the wet-weather gap for €25.

Fitting the titanium rear frame is easy if your main frame's hinge tube already has its bushes fitted and reamed. If they're missing or worn, that step needs Brompton's reamer and is a dealer job.

Before you order

  • Carbon fork, rim brake. The listing mentions 355 as well as 349. Ask the seller whether a Brompton caliper reaches a 349 rim, and check the caliper hole takes Brompton's 8 mm recessed nut. Also confirm steerer thread length and the fold hook mount.
  • BrommiePlus 3×2 wheelset. Confirm the rear hub is the BWR with the two-sprocket driver. You still need Brompton's derailleur-version chain tensioner, chain pusher and left shifter.
  • Hinge bushes. Push the spindle through your main frame's hinge tube. If it's missing bushes or rattles, have a dealer fit and ream them before you fit the rear frame.
  • Derailleur on the old rear frame. If you pick a 5-speed derailleur set, ask BrommiePlus which hanger and derailleur fit a Superlight Ti rear frame.
  • Import costs. BrommiePlus ships from Taiwan: expect 20% import VAT plus up to 4.7% duty. AliExpress shows VAT included but warns that import charges still apply.
  • Weigh as you go. Put each part on a kitchen scale and update the calculator.

How the weights are estimated

The reference is a factory Brompton M2E-X: steel main frame and M stem, titanium fork and rear frame, 2-speed, no mudguards or rack, reported at about 9.7 kg. Your frame matches it part for part, so each option is added as a change against that bike's stock parts. Two other factory figures agree with it: the S6E-X based CHPT3 edition at 10.3 kg, and a gap of about 0.73 kg between Brompton's 2-speed and 6-speed systems.
